# Garments AR

Static web AR site: one page per garment, opened from a printed QR code. camera AR with the 8th Wall engine, plus the intro video and zoomable sewing patterns as overlays (works on iPhone Safari and Android Chrome without ARCore/ARKit).

- `docs/` is the published site (GitHub Pages, branch `main`, folder `/docs`). Each garment lives at `docs/<id>/` with its files in `docs/models/<id>/`.
- Each garment page **is** the AR page (`docs/<id>/index.html`, media baked in): the camera opens first, the garment is placed on the floor (tap), pinch to resize, swipe on it to rotate, and the bottom buttons **View Video** and **View Patterns** open overlays whose close buttons return to the same AR session.

## Adding a garment

1. Create `source/<id>/` (git-ignored, never published) and drop in three files, any names:
   the CLO export `*.glb`, the intro video `*.mp4`, the pattern image `*.png` / `*.jpg`.
2. Run `python make_garment.py <id> "<Name>" "<one-line description>"`.
   It exports the model (Draco GLB + poster, hair detected and fixed automatically, 0.9 scale, 250k-triangle budget),
   encodes the video to a 2 MB 720p streaming copy, copies the pattern, adds the row to `models.json`,
   rebuilds the site and writes `qr/<id>-<name>.png`. Options: `--scale`, `--cloth`, `--skin`,
   `--budget`, `--hair Name1,Name2` to force hair cards, `--skip-model`, `--skip-video`, `--usdz` (Apple Quick Look file; the site does not use it).
3. Check the printed `MAT` / `PLAN` lines and `docs/models/<id>/poster.webp` (or `qa/<id>/front.webp` and `back.webp`, or `docs/qa.html?m=<id>` on the local preview), then `git add -A && git commit -m "Add garment <id>" && git push`.
   The page is live at `<base_url>/<id>/` about a minute later; print the QR from `qr/`.

Requirements on the machine: Blender 5.x (path in `make_garment.py` or the `BLENDER` env var), Python 3 with `pip install qtfaststart qrcode[pil]`.

- `docs/ar/app.js` is the shared script behind every garment page (8th Wall's free open-source engine, its own SLAM, no ARCore/ARKit needed, + three.js): tap to place, pinch to resize, swipe on the garment to rotate. `docs/ar/index.html` is the same page driven by `?m=<id>` and the generated `docs/models.json`. The engine binary requires the copyright notice and licence link that the generator keeps in every page's HTML.
- `models.json` lists the garments. `python build_site.py` regenerates the pages.
- `tools/glb_to_web.py` (called by `make_garment.py`) turns a CLO `.glb` into `model.glb` (Draco) and `poster.webp`. Every export differs (mannequins from 95k to 2.4M faces, hundreds of topstitch meshes, hair as alpha cards or solid geometry), so geometry is reduced against a global budget of 250k triangles (`--budget`): hair cards and tiny parts (eyes, lashes) are kept intact, the mannequin body is capped at 100k, hair modelled as solid strands (exported as blend without alpha; garment 4 has 540k faces of it) gets its own pool of whatever the budget leaves so it can never starve the face, all topstitch trims together at 40k (1.5k each), flat dense artefacts (shoe soles) collapse to 2k, and the cloth gets the rest (at most 35% of the original). Exports whose topstitch meshes are too heavy to import at all (garment 6: 5,574 embroidered motifs, 24.6M triangles; garment 9) have them baked into the fabric textures instead by `tools/stitch_bake.py`: the trim vertices are read straight from the .glb, dropped onto the nearest fabric face and painted in the thread colour into a per-material atlas laid out in CLO's pattern space (the UVs are the flat pattern pieces in millimetres, so they never overlap). The mannequin under the garments is cleaned up in three passes (head, hair and eyes are never touched): skin that pokes through the fabric or lies closer than 6 mm under it is pushed back to 6 mm under the innermost fabric layer (CLO exports often leave the back of a tight skirt penetrating; the push only follows fabric in the near half of a body part, so thin parts and the far side are safe); then every skin vertex is tested for visibility from ~40 directions around the model (from the sides, up to 45 degrees above and slightly below, against opaque cloth and the mannequin itself, from both sides of the surface so oddly wound boots cannot open holes), and regions nobody can see are deleted with a one-ring rim kept. `qa/<id>/front.webp` and `back.webp` show the result. In the AR page the mannequin is additionally drawn a few millimetres further back in the depth buffer (a depth offset on its materials), so the fabric wins wherever the two surfaces still nearly touch. Hair cards (alpha-textured materials in the head region) become a matte double-sided alpha cutout at 0.3 with hard-alpha textures; solid hair exported as "blend" is made opaque. Opaque PNG textures are re-saved as JPEG. Read the printed `MAT ...` classification and `PLAN ...` lines for each new garment; `--hair Name1,Name2` forces materials to be treated as hair cards.
- `tools/usd_fix.py` is called by the export script: it patches the USD (hair alpha cutout, matte, double-sided meshes) and packages the `.usdz` with Pixar's packager. It runs on Blender's bundled `pxr`; on a machine without it, `pip install usd-core`.
- `python serve.py` previews `docs/` locally on port 8765.
- `python make_qr.py https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/` writes one printable QR PNG per garment into `qr/`.
